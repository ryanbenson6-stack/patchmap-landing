The whole point

When it’s live, the show is in the room — not in anyone’s head.

When the show only lives in people’s heads, things slip — not because anyone’s careless, but because everyone’s working from their own version of it. Open a Live Room and there’s one version in front of everyone, live on their own phone: the stage tech sees the stage, monitors see monitor world, musicians see their channel and can ask for help.

Flag a problem on the exact channel and it routes to whoever can fix it — spotted, seen, resolved, in front of everyone.

Whether it’s a crew you’ve run with for years or one you met tonight, everyone’s finally working from the same thing.

None of this works on a messy document. It works because the show already lives in PatchMap as one clean source of truth — build it once, and the room runs on it.

Week after week

Press next show. Everything comes with you.

Hit Instance Forward and PatchMap carries the whole show to the next date — patch, gear, wireless, notes, all of it. No duplicating a file, no erasing last week and hoping you remembered everything that changed.

It isn’t a copy. The new date stays linked to the one before it, so what you end up with is a show with a history rather than a folder full of near-identical versions with dates in the filenames.

Which is the shape most of the work actually has. The same room every Sunday, the same band every Friday, the same tour in a different building. Ninety per cent of it is the same every time — and it’s the other ten per cent that bites you at soundcheck.

Do that a few times and you don’t have a document any more. You have a record.
